# Reagent Notes: Tomato Protein, RNA and DNA Extraction (2026-07-20)

**Date:** 2026-07-20
**Type:** reference notes (no bench work)
**Covers:** reagents used in the protein (2026-05-28, 2026-06-24), RNA (2026-05-29, 2026-06-25) and DNA (2026-06-18) extractions

The protocols are in the logs listed above. For each reagent, this page gives the formula and molecular weight (MW), its role and concentration in the protocol, a short note on how it works, and handling and safety points.

## 1. Protein extraction

Buffer (2026-05-28): 20 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 1 mM EDTA, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% Triton X-100, 5 mM DTT, 0.1% SDS, 1X protease inhibitor.

### Tris-HCl (tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane hydrochloride)

* **Formula, MW:** C4H11NO3·HCl, 157.60 g/mol.
* **Role:** pH buffer, 20 mM, pH 7.5.
* **Properties:** pKa 8.07 at 25 °C. The pKa changes by about −0.031 per °C, so the pH rises when the solution is cooled to 4 °C.
* **How it works:** keeps the pH stable during lysis, for example against acids released from vacuoles.
* **Handling:** mild skin and eye irritant. Adjust the pH at the working temperature, or correct for the temperature shift.

### EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, disodium salt)

* **Formula, MW:** C10H14N2Na2O8·2H2O, 372.24 g/mol.
* **Role:** chelator, 1 mM (stock at pH 8.0).
* **Properties:** hexadentate chelator; binds divalent cations (Mg2+, Ca2+, Zn2+, Fe2+).
* **How it works:** removes the free divalent cations that metalloproteases and nucleases need.
* **Handling:** EDTA dissolves fully only when the pH is brought to about 8.0 with NaOH. Eye and respiratory irritant.

### Sodium chloride (NaCl)

* **Formula, MW:** NaCl, 58.44 g/mol.
* **Role:** ionic strength, 150 mM.
* **Properties:** dissociates fully into Na+ and Cl−; solubility about 360 g/L at 20 °C.
* **How it works:** a near-physiological salt concentration keeps proteins in solution ("salting in") and reduces non-specific aggregation.
* **Handling:** keep the container closed (absorbs moisture); not hazardous under normal laboratory use.

### Triton X-100

* **Formula, MW:** C14H22O(C2H4O)n, n ≈ 9–10; about 625 g/mol.
* **Role:** non-ionic detergent, 0.1% (v/v).
* **Properties:** hydrophilic polyethylene oxide chain and aromatic hydrophobic group; critical micelle concentration (CMC) 0.2–0.9 mM (0.015–0.06% v/v).
* **How it works:** solubilizes membrane lipids while leaving most non-covalent protein interactions intact, which releases membrane-bound and cytosolic proteins.
* **Handling:** viscous; pipette slowly. Toxic to aquatic organisms and a suspected endocrine disruptor; dispose of as chemical waste.

### DTT (dithiothreitol)

* **Formula, MW:** C4H10O2S2, 154.25 g/mol.
* **Role:** reducing agent, 5 mM.
* **Properties:** dithiol (Cleland's reagent); redox potential E°′ = −0.33 V at pH 7.0. When oxidized, it forms a stable six-membered ring with an internal disulfide bond.
* **How it works:** reduces disulfide bonds (–S–S–) to free thiols (–SH), which prevents oxidation-induced aggregation.
* **Handling:** oxidizes quickly in solution exposed to air; store stocks at −20 °C and add fresh to the buffer just before use. Unpleasant odour; harmful if swallowed; skin and respiratory irritant.

### SDS (sodium dodecyl sulfate)

* **Formula, MW:** C12H25SO4Na, 288.38 g/mol.
* **Role:** anionic, denaturing detergent, 0.1% (w/v).
* **Properties:** 12-carbon tail and sulfate head group; CMC about 8 mM (0.23% w/v) in water at 25 °C, lower in salt buffers.
* **How it works:** disrupts non-covalent protein structure and coats proteins with negative charge.
* **Handling:** the fine powder is an inhalation hazard; weigh it in a fume hood. SDS precipitates below about 10 °C and in solutions with high K+ concentrations.

### Liquid nitrogen (LN2)

* **Formula, MW:** N2, 28.01 g/mol.
* **Role:** freezing tissue for grinding.
* **Properties:** boiling point −195.79 °C (77.36 K); expands about 694-fold from liquid to gas.
* **How it works:** freezes tissue quickly, which stops metabolism and protease activity and makes the tissue brittle for grinding.
* **Handling:** cryogenic burns; wear insulated gloves and eye protection. Risk of asphyxiation in poorly ventilated rooms. Never close LN2 in a sealed container.

## 2. RNA extraction

Reagents: DEPC-treated water and plasticware, TRIzol, chloroform, isopropanol, ethanol; for the DNase clean-up, PCI, 3 M sodium acetate, glycogen and ethanol (2026-05-29).

### DEPC (diethyl pyrocarbonate)

* **Formula, MW:** C6H10O5, 162.14 g/mol.
* **Role:** inactivates RNases in water and on plasticware.
* **Properties:** electrophilic; density 1.12 g/mL at 20 °C. Hydrolyses to ethanol and CO2, faster during autoclaving (121 °C, 15–20 min).
* **How it works:** carbethoxylates histidine and other nucleophilic residues of RNases, which inactivates them irreversibly.
* **Handling:** suspected carcinogen; volatile, moisture-sensitive lachrymator. It must be destroyed by autoclaving before use: residual DEPC carbethoxylates adenine residues in RNA and inhibits reverse transcription and other enzymatic steps.

### TRIzol (guanidinium thiocyanate and phenol)

* **Composition:** single-phase acidic solution (pH about 4.0) of guanidinium thiocyanate (MW 118.16 g/mol) and phenol (C6H5OH, MW 94.11 g/mol), with buffer salts and a red dye; density about 1.2 g/mL.
* **Role:** lysis, protein denaturation and separation of RNA.
* **How it works:** guanidinium thiocyanate is a strong chaotrope; it denatures proteins and inactivates RNases at once. At this acidic pH, DNA and denatured proteins move into the interphase and organic phase after chloroform is added, while RNA stays in the upper aqueous phase.
* **Handling:** toxic and corrosive, rapidly absorbed through skin. Work in a fume hood with nitrile gloves.

### Chloroform

* **Formula, MW:** CHCl3, 119.38 g/mol.
* **Role:** phase separation.
* **Properties:** dense (1.49 g/cm³ at 25 °C), volatile; immiscible with water, forms the lower layer.
* **How it works:** increases the density difference between the phases, which gives a sharp interface; extracts lipids and remaining hydrophobic proteins into the organic phase.
* **Handling:** suspected carcinogen, liver toxicant and central nervous system depressant. Use only in a fume hood. Attacks some plastics, such as polystyrene.

### Isopropanol (2-propanol)

* **Formula, MW:** C3H8O, 60.10 g/mol.
* **Role:** precipitation of RNA (1:1 with the aqueous phase).
* **Properties:** dielectric constant about 18.3 (water: about 80); miscible with water.
* **How it works:** lowers the dielectric constant, so that cations can neutralize the phosphate backbone and the RNA precipitates.
* **Handling:** highly flammable; serious eye irritant; vapours cause dizziness and drowsiness.

### Ethanol

* **Formula, MW:** C2H5OH, 46.07 g/mol.
* **Role:** 70–75% for washing pellets; 100% for precipitation with sodium acetate.
* **Properties:** dielectric constant about 24.5 at 25 °C; boiling point 78.37 °C.
* **How it works:** 70–75% ethanol dissolves salts (Na+, guanidinium) left in the pellet without dissolving the RNA. 100% ethanol with monovalent cations precipitates nucleic acids.
* **Handling:** highly flammable; store in a flammables cabinet. Dry the pellet before resuspension, because residual ethanol inhibits enzymes.

### PCI (phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol, 25:24:1)

* **Composition:** phenol, chloroform and isoamyl alcohol (C5H12O, MW 88.15 g/mol); density above 1.2 g/mL.
* **Role:** removes the DNase (and other proteins) after digestion.
* **How it works:** phenol denatures proteins at the interface; chloroform helps the phases separate and removes traces of phenol from the aqueous phase; isoamyl alcohol reduces foaming and stabilizes the interphase.
* **Handling:** corrosive and toxic; causes chemical burns and is absorbed through skin. Take the reagent from the lower (organic) phase below the protective aqueous layer in the bottle, with suitable tips, in a fume hood.

### Sodium acetate

* **Formula, MW:** CH3COONa, 82.03 g/mol.
* **Role:** salt for ethanol precipitation (3 M stock, pH 5.2).
* **How it works:** Na+ neutralizes the negative charges of the phosphate backbone, reducing repulsion so that ethanol can precipitate the RNA.
* **Handling:** mild skin, eye and respiratory irritant; absorbs moisture, keep closed.

### Glycogen

* **Formula:** (C6H10O5)n; branched glucose polymer (α-1→4 linkages with α-1→6 branches).
* **Role:** carrier for precipitating small amounts of RNA.
* **Properties:** water-soluble; insoluble at high alcohol concentrations.
* **How it works:** co-precipitates with nucleic acids and forms a visible pellet, which reduces loss when the supernatant is removed.
* **Handling:** not hazardous; generally does not interfere with reverse transcription, PCR or A260/A280 readings. Store at −20 °C in sterile aliquots.

## 3. DNA extraction (CTAB)

Reagents: CTAB buffer, β-mercaptoethanol, PCI, isopropanol; 70% ethanol for cleaning the drill tip (2026-06-18).

### CTAB (cetyltrimethylammonium bromide)

* **Formula, MW:** C19H42BrN, 364.45 g/mol.
* **Role:** cationic detergent for lysis of plant tissue.
* **Properties:** quaternary ammonium surfactant with a 16-carbon chain; CMC about 1 mM at 25 °C. Dissolves well only when warm.
* **How it works:** solubilizes membranes during lysis at 65 °C. At high NaCl (above about 0.7 M), CTAB–nucleic acid complexes stay in solution.
* **Handling:** causes serious eye damage and skin irritation; toxic to aquatic organisms. It precipitates below about 15 °C; warm the stock before use.

### β-Mercaptoethanol (2-mercaptoethanol, BME)

* **Formula, MW:** C2H6OS, 78.13 g/mol.
* **Role:** reducing agent in the CTAB buffer, against oxidation of polyphenols. (It was left out in 2026-07-28.)
* **Properties:** thiol with a strong odour and high vapour pressure; reduces disulfide bonds.
* **How it works:** inhibits polyphenol oxidase and keeps plant polyphenols reduced, so that they do not form quinones that bind to DNA.
* **Handling:** toxic by inhalation, skin contact and ingestion; strong odour. Dispense only in a fume hood; add fresh to the CTAB buffer just before grinding.

### PCI (phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol, 25:24:1)

* **Role:** two extractions to remove proteins from the lysate.
* **How it works:** as above; high-molecular-weight genomic DNA stays in the upper aqueous phase.
* **Handling:** as above.

### Isopropanol

* **Role:** precipitation of genomic DNA (1:1 with the aqueous phase).
* **How it works:** as above; cations (Na+, K+) neutralize the phosphate backbone and the DNA precipitates.
* **Handling:** flammable; vapours cause drowsiness and dizziness.

### Ethanol, 70%

* **Role:** wiping the drill tip between samples.
* **How it works:** removes tissue residue from the tip and reduces carry-over between samples (ethanol does not degrade DNA).
* **Handling:** flammable; keep away from sparks of the drill motor.
